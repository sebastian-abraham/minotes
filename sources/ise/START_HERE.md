## How these kits are written

Each concept is a five-part block, and the order matters:

1. **In plain English** — what this actually is, in two or three sentences, with one comparison. No jargon allowed here.
2. **The detail** — the mechanism, the classification, the numbers. Short bullets.
3. **What to write** — the answer as it goes on the paper, at the mark value.
4. **Mark anchor** — the one fact that proves you know the topic rather than half-remember it.
5. **Exam trap** — the pair of things that get confused, phrased as a decision.

If a deck slide was a picture, the kit says so and gives the standard content, marked **(standard)**. Nothing is invented.

## The five modules in one line each

1. **Why safety exists, and who owns it** — accident theories (Domino, human factors, systems), the safety officer, the committee, the union and the law.
2. **What you wear and how you measure** — PPE selection, four performance rates with formulas, housekeeping and 5S, work permits, confined spaces.
3. **Construction** — excavation, shoring, underpinning, ladders, scaffolds, tunnelling, blasting, demolition, NBC provisions, ergonomics.
4. **Machines and loads** — point of operation, guarding, power presses, machine tools, welding, manual and mechanical handling, ropes and slings.
5. **Finding hazards before they bite** — fire classes and extinguishers, Dow index, PHA, HAZOP, chemical hazards, MSDS. **Biggest bank in the subject (47 questions)** — do it early, not last.

## Order of attack

1. **M5** — largest bank, most technical (Dow, HAZOP), and the easiest to score if you learn the procedure.
2. **M2** — formulas are free marks, and PPE/housekeeping questions are always "list and explain".
3. **M1** — pure definitions and lists; the Domino theory is asked in some form almost every time.
4. **M4** — guard types and handling rules; learn the classification tables rather than prose.
5. **M3** — construction safety is the most scenario-driven; practise the demolition/high-rise scenario answers.
